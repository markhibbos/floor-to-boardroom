One Life

Privacy policy

Last updated: 9 October 2026

In short: One Life does not collect, store or share any personal data. There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking. Your games are saved on your own phone or computer, and nobody else can see them.

What the game stores, and where

Everything the game remembers is kept on your device:

  • your saved games, your character's name and look, and your photo album;
  • your hall of fame, trophies and collection book;
  • settings such as sound on or off.

In the iPhone and Android apps this is kept in the app's own storage. On the website it is kept in your browser's local storage. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else. If you delete the app or clear your browser data, it is gone.

What we collect

Nothing. We don't ask for your name, email address, location, contacts or any other personal information, and the game has no sign-in.

Sharing

When you choose to share a life record, an obituary or a front page, the game opens your phone's share sheet (or copies a link). What you share, and who you share it with, is up to you. A shared life link contains a summary of that one game life, written into the link itself. It isn't stored anywhere by us.

If you add a friend's shared life to your game, their life summary is saved only on your device.

Children

The game does not collect data from anyone, including children. It includes grown-up themes (work, money, relationships, alcohol, simulated gambling and death), so it is rated for teenagers and adults.

Hosting

The iPhone and Android apps make no internet connections at all: the game and its fonts are built in. The website version is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may see technical details such as your IP address when it delivers the page, under its own privacy statement. We don't receive or use that information, and no game data is included.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.

Contact

Questions about privacy? Open an issue at github.com/markhibbos/floor-to-boardroom.
